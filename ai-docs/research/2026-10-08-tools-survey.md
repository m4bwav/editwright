# Editing tools survey, October 2026

Read this when you design an editwright feature, write its README pitch, or need to know what a competitor already does.
It covers commercial editing tools for writers, public agent skills and plugins for editing, what writers complain about, and how each tool treats the author's voice.

All sources were checked on 2026-10-08 unless a row says otherwise.
Ideas are paraphrased. No text was copied from any product.
Reddit could not be fetched directly, so writer complaints come from search snippets, review sites and forums that quote Reddit.

## Method and limits

- Web search and page fetches on 2026-10-08. GitHub data came from `gh repo view` and `gh search repos` the same day.
- Star counts and last-push dates are a snapshot on 2026-10-08.
- Several product pages do not say whether AI rewrites text or trains on manuscripts. Where a page is silent, this note says so.
- Review aggregator sites (tooliverse, eesel, builtwritten and similar) are weak sources. They are used only where no primary page exists, and are marked "secondary".

## Big picture in one paragraph

The market has split into two camps.
Generators (Sudowrite, Novelcrafter, NovelAI, Squibler, ProWritingAid Sparks, Grammarly Paraphraser) write or rewrite prose for you.
Analysers (Marlowe, Fictionary, AutoCrit's reports, Plottr's off-by-default AI) diagnose and leave the words alone.
Only Grammarly tracks who wrote which words, and it does so for teachers checking students, not for authors keeping their own record.
In the open-source agent space, one small plugin (APODICTIC) already takes the strict "never rewrite" line for developmental editing.
Nobody combines line-level diagnosis, a no-rewrite rule, and an author-owned ledger of AI-written words.

## Commercial tools

### Grammarly (now a product of Superhuman)

Status: alive. The parent company renamed itself Superhuman on 2025-10-29. Grammarly stays as a product brand inside a suite with Coda, Superhuman Mail and Superhuman Go.
Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251029236844/en (checked 2026-10-08), https://fastcompany.co.za/tech/2025-10-30-exploring-grammarly-s-transition-to-superhuman/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Agents (August 2025): eight agents shipped in a new writing surface called "docs". They are Reader Reactions, Proofreader, AI Grader, Citation Finder, Expert Review, AI Detector, Plagiarism Checker and Paraphraser.
Reader Reactions predicts what a named reader (a professor, a manager) will take away, what they will ask, and where they will get confused.
Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250818186419/en/Grammarly-Launches-Specialized-AI-Agents-and-Writing-Surface-for-Students-and-Professionals (checked 2026-10-08).

Expert Review scandal (March 2026): the Expert Review agent gave feedback "inspired by" named real people, such as Stephen King and Steven Pinker, without their consent. Journalist Julia Angwin filed a proposed class action on 2026-03-11. The CEO pulled the feature the same day.
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/grammarly-faces-lawsuit-over-ai-tool-using-academics-identities (checked 2026-10-08), https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/grammarly-sued-over-feature-touting-unwitting-expert-feedback (checked 2026-10-08).

Authorship (the most relevant feature for editwright):
- Launched in beta in October 2024. It records, as you write, whether text was typed, pasted from a known web source, pasted from an unknown source, generated by AI, or changed by Grammarly's own AI rephrasing.
- It can replay how a document was built, step by step, and produces a shareable report with the percentage of human and AI text.
- Works in Google Docs (browser extension) and Word (desktop app). The April 2025 update added Word and automatic citation of pasted sources.
- Source: https://www.grammarly.com/blog/academic-writing/grammarly-authorship-just-got-a-major-update/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/29548735595405 (checked 2026-10-08).
- Laundering flaw (November 2025): text run through Grammarly's own "Humanize" tool showed under a green "Typed by a Human" heading, with AI involvement only in fine print. Source: https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/11/06/how-grammarly-launders-ai-generated-content/ (checked 2026-10-08).
- Fix (December 2025): AI-rewritten text moved to an "AI-Generated or Rephrased with AI" subcategory, filed under "Copied from a Source". Plain grammar fixes still count as human-typed. Source: https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/12/11/grammarly-updates-authorship-improves-labeling/ (checked 2026-10-08).
- Agent attribution (2026-03-10): in "docs", Authorship is on by default and now says which agent was used and for what: research support, content generation, or revision feedback. Over 5 million reports have been generated by students. Source: https://www.grammarly.com/blog/company/superhuman-authorship-docs/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice features: Grammarly builds a "voice profile" from your writing and can rewrite text in that voice. Source: https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46242251232781-Introducing-voice-features (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints:
- Flags deliberate fragments, odd punctuation and style choices as errors; strips voice. Summarised from r/writing in https://eesel.ai/blog/grammarly-alternatives (secondary, checked 2026-10-08) and https://jillebstein.substack.com/p/whats-the-difference-between-grammarly (checked 2026-10-08).
- Students say Grammarly-only edits got flagged as AI by Turnitin. Source: https://turnitin.forumbee.com/t/g9hcsxv/ai-detection-and-grammarly?pg=2 (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: accept or reject per suggestion; generative rewrites are one click away; Authorship records origin but is built for teachers, not authors.

Strongest idea to borrow: a per-span origin label (typed, pasted, AI-generated, AI-edited) with a replayable history, and labels that cannot be laundered. Also Reader Reactions: predict what a named reader will misunderstand.

### ProWritingAid

Status: alive.
Features: 25+ line-editing reports (grammar, sticky sentences, overused words, repeats, sentence length, dialogue, pacing, clichés, sensory, consistency). Manuscript Analysis and a Virtual Beta Reader for whole books. Chapter Critique, Character Analysis. Generative "Sparks" (since November 2023) and "Rephrase".
Source: https://prowritingaid.com/features (checked 2026-10-08).
Manuscript Analysis takes 6,000 to 300,000 words. Source: https://help.prowritingaid.com/article/318-what-is-manuscript-analysis (checked 2026-10-08).
Sparks modes include fluency, readability, sensory, expand from notes, and changing person or tense. Source: https://www.sentisight.ai/prowritingaid-an-accurate-ai-grammar-checker (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).
The features page says nothing about voice or training on user text.

Writer complaints:
- A flood of suggestions; one forum user said about one real problem per thirty flags. Source: https://tildes.net/~creative/1nck/thoughts_on_prowritingaid (checked 2026-10-08).
- Even in "Creative" style mode it marks stylistic choices as errors. Source: same tildes thread and https://www.eesel.ai/en/blog/grammarly-alternatives (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).
- Credits sold on top of a premium licence, slow on large files, refund friction. Source: https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/prowritingaid.com (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: suggestions are accept or reject; Sparks and Rephrase rewrite on request; no record of which words were AI-written.

Strongest idea to borrow: many narrow, named reports (sticky sentences, echoes, sentence-length variety) that each count something the writer can see, rather than one vague "improve this".

### Sudowrite

Status: alive. Proprietary fiction model Muse (Muse 1.5 public mid-2025). Story Bible holds characters, world, plot and timeline, read by the model when it writes. Write reads up to 20,000 words of manuscript before continuing. Series Folder spans books.
Source: https://sudowrite.com/blog/sudowrite-muse-the-first-ai-writer-built-specifically-for-fiction/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://sudowrite.com/blog/how-sudowrite-works/ (checked 2026-10-08).
Pricing: credit tiers from $10 to $44 a month on annual billing; credits reset monthly except on Max. Source: https://ucstrategies.com/news/sudowrite-review-i-tested-the-22-month-ai-against-chatgpt-across-70000-words/ (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: generic or clichéd output that "won't sound fully like you" and needs heavy editing for voice. Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/10016204/Sudowrite/reviews/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://www.unite.ai/ar/sudowrite-review/ (checked 2026-10-08). Credit burn on premium models (same ucstrategies source).

Voice handling: it is a generator. It tries to imitate your voice rather than protect it.

Strongest idea to borrow: the Story Bible as a structured, reusable reference the tool checks against. For editwright that means a style sheet and character facts used to catch continuity errors, not to write.

### Novelcrafter

Status: alive. The Codex is a structured database of characters, places, factions and rules that gets injected into prompts. Bring your own key (OpenRouter, Claude, GPT, Gemini, local Ollama). Character interviews via chat with Codex memory.
Source: https://www.novelcrafter.com/features/codex (checked 2026-10-08), https://openrouter.ai/works-with-openrouter/novelcrafter (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: few public complaints found in search; reviews describe a steep, "Photoshop-like" set-up. Source: https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-for-creative-writing-2026.md (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: generator; the writer chooses the model and prompts.

Strongest idea to borrow: Codex entries that are only sent to the model when the scene mentions them. Editwright can load only the style-sheet entries that matter for the passage under review, which saves tokens.

### AutoCrit

Status: alive. Story Analyzer+ flags pacing, character inconsistency, plot threads and world-building errors. Genre benchmarks refreshed against recent bestsellers. Simulated alpha and beta readers. Also AI prompts for twists and tonal shifts.
Source: https://www.autocrit.com/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://expandsummit2026.heysummit.com/talks/edit-with-ai-how-autocrits-story-analyzer-takes-your-manuscript-to-the-next-level/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: counts adverbs, dialogue tags and passive voice with simple rules; compares you to named bestsellers, which pushes toward a mean. Source: https://kindlepreneur.com/autocrit-review/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://selfpublishing.com/autocrit-review/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: mostly diagnostic, with some generative prompts added.

Strongest idea to borrow: set the genre first, because what counts as a problem depends on it.

### Marlowe (Authors A.I.)

Status: alive. Analytical, not generative. Report covers plot structure, narrative arcs, character, pacing, dialogue balance, theme, genre benchmarks, readability, clichés and repeats, plus comp titles. "No rewrite this chapter button." Manuscripts are never used for training; trained on rights-cleared novels. Reports from $29.95; Basic is free, Pro is $29.95 a month or $199 a year.
Source: https://authors.ai/marlowe/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: little negative in search results; reviews are mostly positive. Source: https://gianacliscaldwell.com/2024/08/19/marlowe-ai-author-tool-review/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: the clearest commercial "we never write" position.

Strongest idea to borrow: say plainly, in the product itself, that there is no rewrite button and why; and state the training and data policy up front.

### Fictionary

Status: alive. StoryTeller ($19 a month, Premium $29) gives AI scene feedback (goal, tension, conflict, word count) and 15 visualisations such as Story Arc and Story Map. StoryCoach ($49) is for editors. The method checks 38 story elements across character, plot and setting.
Source: https://fictionary.co/products/storyteller/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://selfpublishing.com/fictionary-review/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://fictionary.co/journal/overcome-your-writers-block-with-elements-of-a-story (checked 2026-10-08).
Certification: a five-week Certified StoryCoach Editor course of 12 modules ending in a graded full manuscript edit. Source: https://fictionary.co/journal/introducing-fictionary-certified-storycoach-training/ (checked 2026-10-08).
The StoryTeller page does not say whether AI rewrites text or trains on manuscripts.

Writer complaints: price questioned on Goodreads (https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/22201441-is-fictionary-worth-the-price, checked 2026-10-08). Reviews stress it is structure only and must be paired with a line editor.

Voice handling: analysis and visual maps; the writer revises.

Strongest idea to borrow: a fixed, named checklist of scene elements (goal, conflict, entry and exit hooks) scored per scene, so diagnosis is repeatable and teachable. A human certification built on the same checklist shows the checklist is trusted by working editors.

### Hemingway Editor (Plus)

Status: alive. Free editor highlights hard sentences, adverbs, passive voice and weakeners. Plus adds AI "Fix it for me" (one credit each), eight style rewrites and grammar checking. You click "Use suggestion" or ask for another.
Source: https://hemingwayapp.com/hemingway-editor-plus (checked 2026-10-08), https://hemingwayapp.com/help/docs/highlighted-issues (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: pushes all prose toward short and plain. Source: https://originality.ai/pt/blog/hemingway-editor-plus-readability-review (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: the free tier is pure highlighting, which leaves the words alone; Plus adds rewrite buttons.

Strongest idea to borrow: colour-coded highlights by issue type with counts, and a cheap no-AI layer that runs first.

### Lex

Status: alive (founded 2022 by Nathan Baschez; Lex V2 rolled out from April 2025). "Checks" are AI line edits for brevity, grammar, clarity, readability, confidence and active voice. You pick which checks to run on chosen paragraphs and accept or reject each.
Source: https://buttondown.com/lex/archive/introducing-lex-v2/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://buttondown.com/lex/archive/new-in-lex-check-for-brevity-readability-grammar-8714/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Writer complaints: brevity and readability checks flag hundreds of sentences and writers accept under half, because many simplify away needed context. Source: https://adopter.substack.com/p/lex-ai-writter (checked 2026-10-08).

Voice handling: per-suggestion accept or reject, with explanations.

Strongest idea to borrow: let the writer choose which checks to run, and explain every suggestion.

### Plottr

Status: alive. AI is off by default for everyone, paid per use, and never contacts a model until you press a clearly marked button. Designed for brainstorming, the use Amazon does not require you to disclose. 2025 shipped Storysnap (book bibles and virtual beta readers).
Source: https://plottr.com/ai-in-plottr-workflow/ (published 2024-05-13, updated 2024-12-23, checked 2026-10-08), https://plottr.com/plottr-in-2026/ (checked 2026-10-08).

Strongest idea to borrow: AI off by default, behind one visible switch, framed around the KDP line between AI-assisted and AI-generated.

### Squibler

Status: alive; Smart Writer since November 2023 generates and rewrites scenes; claims 20,000 active writers.
Source: https://www.builtwritten.com/blog/squibler-ai-2026 (secondary, checked 2026-10-08), https://reedsy.com/studio/resources/squibler-review (checked 2026-10-08).
Complaints: inconsistent output that needs heavy rewriting (same sources).
Voice handling: generator. Nothing to borrow for editwright beyond the warning.

### NovelAI

Status: alive. Text generation now runs on GLM-4.6 for all tiers, with longer context.
Source: https://mybesh.com/tool/novelai (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).
Voice handling: co-writing generator. Out of scope except as a contrast.

### Storywise

Status: alive (launched March 2024). Sold to publishers and agents, not writers. Reads slush submissions and returns genre, word count, writing quality, structure, marketability, comp titles, a developmental critique, a taste-match score, and an AI-detection score. Humans still decide.
Source: https://publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/95210-book-business-ai-showcase-spines-and-storywise.html (checked 2026-10-08), https://www.ibpa-online.org/page/Storywise (checked 2026-10-08).
Why it matters: submissions are screened for AI text. A writer who can show an honest ledger of AI words has an answer when a detector misfires.

### Spines

Status: alive. AI-plus-human publishing service: editing, proofreading, formatting, cover, distribution, from $1,200 to $5,000; upgrade to a human at any stage.
Source: https://thebookseller.com/news/new-publisher-spines-aims-to-disrupt-industry-by-using-ai-to-publish-8000-books-in-2025-alone (checked 2026-10-08), https://refreshmiami.com/news/spines-raises-6-5m-to-forge-the-future-of-book-publishing/ (checked 2026-10-08).
Not a direct competitor; shows the "AI edits, human signs off" service model.

### Reedsy

No formal public policy on generative AI found. A Reedsy panel said some publishers now add contract clauses where authors state no AI wrote the work, and that Reedsy might ask its professionals to disclose AI use. Reedsy Studio is described as AI-free in a secondary source.
Source: https://reedsy.com/freelancer/ai-in-publishing/ (checked 2026-10-08), https://www.parse.gl/brands/reedsy-com (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).

### Atticus and Scrivener

Atticus is a writing and formatting tool; no AI editing features found. Scrivener has no native AI; no official AI plugin found.
Source: https://ghostwritingsolution.com/blog/22-best-book-writing-software/ (secondary, checked 2026-10-08), https://agrariansolutions.edencreative.co/?p=15089 (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).
Why it matters: many novelists keep manuscripts in Scrivener. The gerunds plugin (below) already extracts `.scriv` projects to Markdown.

### Google Docs (Gemini "Proofread")

Status: alive. "Proofread" replaces the old spelling and grammar check with AI suggestions (concision, active voice, word choice, sentence splitting) in a side panel with accept and reject. Rolled out to most Workspace tiers from 2026-02-04.
Source: https://chromeunboxed.com/google-docs-adds-new-ai-driven-proofreading-option-to-help-you-create-high-quality-writing/ (checked 2026-10-08).

### Microsoft Word (Copilot "Writing suggestions" and Editor)

Status: alive. Copilot reviews a selection or the whole document and suggests additions, organisation and tone. Microsoft says suggestions stay separate from your content so nothing is overwritten; you tick or clear each one.
Source: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/use-writing-suggestions-to-review-content-in-word (checked 2026-10-08).

Strongest idea to borrow (Docs and Word): suggestions live beside the text, never in it, until the author acts.

## Market context that shapes editwright

- Amazon KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated content but not AI-assisted content. Text an AI created counts as generated even after heavy human edits. Editing, error-checking and brainstorming count as assisted. Source: https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/389007/amazon-distinguishes-between-ai-generated-and-ai-a.html (checked 2026-10-08), https://thebookseller.com/news/amazon-revises-kdp-guidelines-to-compel-disclosure-of-ai-content (checked 2026-10-08). A 2026 article reports Amazon now enforcing the rule: https://writeo.app/news/amazon-kdp-ai-disclosure-enforcement-2026 (secondary, checked 2026-10-08).
- NaNoWriMo shut down in April 2025 after a pro-AI statement cost it board members and community trust. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals (checked 2026-10-08). Writers' communities are hostile to AI that writes.

The KDP line is editwright's best argument. If editwright never writes prose, a writer's use of it stays "AI-assisted". The ledger proves it, and counts any exceptions.

Takeaways from the open-source side:
- The two most popular "editing" skills (humanizer, stop-slop) rewrite. They are popular because people want AI-sounding text fixed, not because they protect voice.
- The no-rewrite stance exists (APODICTIC, gerunds) but only at the developmental level. Neither does line-level diagnosis for the author to act on, and neither counts AI-written words.
- Nothing found in awesome-copilot covers fiction or manuscript editing, so a Copilot port would be first there.

## Ideas to borrow, ranked

Ranked by value to editwright's promise (diagnose, author writes, ledger counts).

| Rank | Idea (paraphrased) | From | Why it fits editwright |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Per-span origin labels (typed by author, AI-written, AI-edited, pasted) that a tool's own rewrite cannot relabel as human | Grammarly Authorship, after its December 2025 fix | This is the ledger. Learn from the laundering bug: any AI-touched span stays AI-touched. |
| 2 | A visible, enforced "firewall": name the problem and the class of fix, never supply the words | APODICTIC, gerunds, Marlowe | The core promise. Make it testable, not just stated. |

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Laundering. Grammarly once counted its own AI rewrites as human typing. The ledger must never let an AI-touched span become "author" because the author later accepted or nudged it. Record the original AI words and how many survived.
- Suggestion floods. ProWritingAid and Lex users reject most flags. Cap findings per pass, rank by severity, and let the author pick checks.
- Treating style as error. Fragments, odd punctuation and long sentences are often deliberate. Ask about intent, honour genre, and allow an author style sheet of "this is on purpose" exceptions.
- Regression to the mean. AutoCrit's bestseller comparisons and Hemingway's readability push all prose toward one shape. Diagnose against the book's own contract, not an average.
- Impersonating real editors or authors. Grammarly's Expert Review used real names without consent and was pulled within days under a class action. Never present feedback as from a named person.
- "Fix it for me" buttons. Hemingway Plus, Sparks and Paraphraser make rewriting the easy path. Editwright should have no such path by default.
- Voice cloning as a fix for voice loss. Grammarly's voice profile and Sudowrite's imitation still put AI words on the page. Imitating the voice is not protecting it.
- Silence on data. Several product pages say nothing about training on manuscripts. Say it plainly.
- Vague provenance claims. Percentages without a method invite distrust, as with AI detectors. Publish how the ledger counts.
- Community backlash. NaNoWriMo lost trust over a pro-AI stance. Lead with "the author writes", not with AI.

## The gap editwright fills

No product or public skill found on 2026-10-08 does all three of these together:

1. Line and developmental diagnosis like a professional editor, with suggestions anchored beside the text.
2. A hard rule that the AI does not write the author's prose, enforced and tested, not just promised.
3. An author-owned provenance ledger that counts every AI-written word, survives edits, and maps to the KDP AI-assisted versus AI-generated line.

Grammarly has (3) but for teachers, and it rewrites freely.
Marlowe and Fictionary have (2) in practice but are closed services without a ledger.
APODICTIC and gerunds have (2) as open plugins, but only at the developmental level and with no ledger.
Generators (Sudowrite, Novelcrafter, NovelAI, Squibler) and the popular humanizer skills do the opposite of (2).

Editwright's pitch: an editor that marks the page and never writes on it, plus a receipt that proves it.
