# Levels of edit

Name the level at the start of every job and stay inside it. Sources unless stated: EFA definitions (the-efa.org/editorial-services-definitions) and CIEP, "Proofreading or Editing?" free PDF (ciep.uk, pp. 2-6, 16-17). Details: [editing craft note](../../../ai-docs/research/2026-10-08-editing-craft.md).

## Manuscript assessment (critique)
- Covers: the whole work, read once or twice. Premise, genre, intended reader, top strengths, top issues.
- Stops: no line edits; light illustrative markup at most. The EFA files it under Reading, not Editing.
- Deliverable: report, "usually no more than 10 pages" (janefriedman.com/manuscript-evaluations-what-they-are-and-what-to-expect).

## Developmental (structural, substantive, content)
- Covers: content, organisation, genre fit; plot, character, POV, pacing.
- Stops: no comma fixes, no rewriting. Name the problem and the classes of fix; the author writes.
- Deliverable: editorial letter plus margin comments. Letters can run past 30 pages (same Friedman page).

## Line
- Covers: sentence and paragraph level, "language and style" (EFA): rhythm, clarity, word choice, repetition, voice consistency.
- Stops: no restructuring the plot; never impose the editor's style. CIEP: do not change tone of voice or style unless it is wrong for the intended readership (p. 6).
- Deliverable: anchored suggestions and comments.

## Copy
- Covers: spelling, grammar, usage, punctuation, cross-references, consistency and continuity, house or author style. Queries obvious factual errors. Flags legal risks (libel, copyright, plagiarism).
- Stops (CIEP p. 17): no substantial rewriting, no developing content, no substantial research or fact-checking, no checking references are accurate, no solving legal issues.
- Deliverable: changes, numbered queries, style sheet.

## Proof
- Covers: edited text in final layout. Typos, layout errors, style decisions applied, earlier corrections actually made (compare stages), figure and index consistency.
- Stops (CIEP p. 16): no rephrasing for flow, no changing spelling preferences, no reordering, no adding text.
- Deliverable: marked proof.

## Fact-check
- Covers: every assertion of fact (EFA: a separate research service).
- Stops: edits no prose. editwright flags claims and never asserts a fact from model memory ([nonfiction.md](nonfiction.md)).
- Deliverable: claim table, one row per claim with status and source.

## Sensitivity and authenticity read
- Covers: portrayal of a group: tropes, stereotypes, inaccuracies, offence, and what works (sfwa.org/2024/04/02/what-is-sensitivity-reading).
- Stops: readers advise, authors decide (SFWA). The EFA and SFWA define the role by community membership or lived experience. An AI is neither: flag candidate passages, recommend a human reader, never pose as one.
- Deliverable: report plus comments.

## Order of passes
Big to small: plan and develop, then fix the detail, then finalise (CIEP p. 4). Polishing before structure settles wastes the work. For a book:
1. Read through without marking; bible notes only.
2. Reverse outline and scene sheet.
3. Developmental letter.
4. Author revises.
5. Line pass.
6. Copy pass: first sweep builds the style sheet and records decisions, then a mechanical pass against it (practitioner habit, secondary).
7. Proof on laid-out pages.

Do not line-edit a chapter that may be cut. CIEP: "One pair of eyes is not enough"; copyedit and proof should be separate passes (ciep.uk FAQ).

## Where the bodies disagree
- Line: the EFA sells it alone; CIEP puts the line editor beside the copyeditor, and its copyedit includes "flow, tone and intended readership". Say which is meant.
- Critique: EFA files it under Reading; CIEP puts it in "plan and develop" with developmental. Both: no markup.
- Sensitivity vs authenticity: the EFA splits them by who reads (outsider vs community member); SFWA uses "sensitivity" for the lived-experience role; Chicago 18 (2.54) names both (paid text unread).
- Fact-check: CIEP's copyeditor queries only "obvious factual inaccuracies"; the EFA treats checking as its own service.
- Copyedit depth: Chicago 2.53 reportedly splits mechanical from substantive copyediting (secondary, via Wikipedia).
- ACES publishes no level taxonomy that could be found; use it for query craft only.
