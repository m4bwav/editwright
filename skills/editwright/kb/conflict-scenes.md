# Conflict scenes

A lens for the developmental or line pass on any scene where people argue, dodge or confront. It comes from a self-help book on high-stakes talk among real people (Patterson and others, *Crucial Conversations*, 3rd edition), not from fiction craft, so using it on a story is inference: a book rule, unverified on our tools.

## Tag each line

Under stress people slide one of two ways: toward silence or toward force. Tag each spoken line (or beat of action that does the talking) with one of six behaviours, or with a repair:

- Silence: **masking** (saying less than they mean, sarcasm, sugar-coating), **avoiding** (changing the subject, talking around the real issue), **withdrawing** (leaving the talk or the room).
- Force: **controlling** (pushing a view, cutting off, piling on), **labeling** (sticking a name on the other person to dismiss them), **attacking** (wanting the other to hurt or lose).
- Repair: a line that makes it safe to keep talking, such as apologising for the real harm, saying what they do and do not mean, asking what the other wants, or naming a shared aim.

Keep the tags in the working notes; never write them into the author's text.

## What to flag

- **Every line is the same type.** A scene of twelve attacks, or twelve dodges, has no turn in it. Query whether someone should shift: a mask that cracks into an attack, an attack that meets a withdrawal, a repair that fails or lands.
- **No movement across the scene.** Compare the first third with the last. If the mix of tags is the same, the scene may restate its conflict instead of changing it.
- **A repair with no cost.** One line of apology that settles a long fight often reads false. Ask what the repair costs the speaker.
- **Behaviour against character.** A character set up as someone who goes quiet who suddenly attacks needs a cause on the page, or the reader will feel the author's hand.

Report as questions with the quoted line and its tag, never as rewritten dialogue. A same-type scene can be the point (a cold war, a pile-on); ask before treating it as a fault.
