# Short stories

## Length bands
| Band | Words | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flash | usually under 1,000 (markets vary: 500 to 1,500) | flashfictiononline.com (500 to 1,000 for them) |
| Short story | under 7,500 | nebulas.sfwa.org/nebula-rules |
| Novelette | 7,500 to under 17,500 | same |
| Novella | 17,500 to under 40,000 | same |
| Novel | 40,000 and up | same |

The market's own limit wins. Check the band before anything else and report a mismatch as finding 1.

## Compression questions
Synthesised from Le Guin, Saunders and The Writer; judgement, not quotes.
- What is the one central change? Does every scene serve it?
- Does it start as late as a reader can follow? Is backstory fed by the clause, not the page?
- Crowding: is each early detail paid off, or does it colour the end?
- Leaping: which transitions walk where they could jump?
- Does the ending answer the question the opening raised, even obliquely?
- In flash: do the title and the last line carry their weight?
- First 500 words: does something happen to someone, or is it setting and backstory?

## Why slush readers reject
The Writer (2024-07-12, writermag.com/?p=56828):
- long openings with backstory before action
- endings disconnected from the story's promises
- no emotional beats
- a main character who is not active
- wrong market
- cliché topics
- "nothing happens": no change

Clarkesworld "hard sells" (clarkesworldmagazine.com/submissions): undead, standard vampire or werewolf tropes, sentient animals or weapons, children finding significant objects outdoors, RPG adventures, pun-driven humour, protagonists defined only by intelligence or eccentricity.

Slush practice: readers often stop within the first page. Common opening faults are paragraphs of weather, room or furniture, and first sentences overloaded with stuff (search synthesis incl. Apex Magazine; unverified in full).

## Magazine and contest notes that change the edit
- Clarkesworld: 1,000 to 22,000 words, "no exceptions". Rejects work "translated, written, developed, or assisted by these tools"; may ban.
- Asimov's and Analog: will not consider submissions "written, developed, or assisted by these tools" (asimovs.com/contact-us/writers-guidelines).
- Uncanny: no submissions written with AI; undisclosed use leads to a ban (uncannymagazine.com/submissions).
- Writers of the Future: work cannot be generated "in whole or in part" by AI (writersofthefuture.com/contest-rules-writers).
- Nebula: LLM-written work ineligible; any LLM use in the writing process must be disclosed and disqualifies; may catch LLM-backed grammar checkers (secondary report).
- None of these defines whether an AI grammar checker counts as "assisted". Treat even suggestions as a risk the author must weigh.

Consequences for editwright:
- Ask the target market first. Check its AI policy before showing any wording at all.
- Short fiction runs at 0 AI-written words ([ai-text-rules.md](ai-text-rules.md)). Diagnose and query; never hand over sentences.
- Tell the author plainly that strict markets may count even diagnosis-only help as "assisted"; the decision to use editwright for such a submission is theirs.
