# Line, copy and proof passes

Scope limits per level: [levels.md](levels.md). Sources: [editing craft note](../../../ai-docs/research/2026-10-08-editing-craft.md), [tools survey](../../../ai-docs/research/2026-10-08-tools-survey.md).

## Line pass
- Told where showing would serve, or shown where one line of telling would serve better? (Browne & King ch. 1)
- Narration explaining what the scene already showed? (Gardner)
- Unbridged psychic-distance jumps inside a paragraph? (Gardner)
- Dialogue mechanics: ornate tags, adverbs, too many or too few beats? (Browne & King)
- Unintended repetition of a word, gesture or idea? (Browne & King, Le Guin)
- Slow page with no inner pull? (Maass, microtension)
- Read aloud: does rhythm snag, does sentence length go monotonous? (Le Guin)
- Where would a first-time reader's interest drop? (Saunders)
- Every suggestion offered, not imposed, and keeps the author's diction (CIEP p. 6).

## Copy pass
- Style sheet first: references, numbers, punctuation, word list, characters, places, timeline, world terms, deliberate deviations ([deliverables.md](deliverables.md)).
- Spelling, grammar, usage, punctuation against the sheet.
- Continuity: ages, eye colour, travel times, days of the week, who knows what.
- Dialogue and thought styling per Chicago 18 fiction guidance or the author's stated style.
- Query obvious factual errors; do no research. Flag legal risks; do not solve them.
- Queries valid, clear, concise, humble, numbered, polite (ACES).
- No substantial rewriting (CIEP p. 17).

## Proof pass
- Typos, layout errors, style decisions applied.
- Earlier corrections actually made (compare stages).
- Figures and tables numbered and placed; index consistent.
- Out of scope: rephrasing for flow, changing spelling preferences, restructuring (CIEP p. 16).

## Reading ew.py stats
Counts are prompts to look, never verdicts. A count names where to read; the finding is what the reading shows.
- Sentence lengths: look for monotony in a passage (Le Guin, read aloud). Do not push toward short and plain; that is Hemingway's known bias and drags prose to one shape.
- Close repeats: check each against intent. An echo can be deliberate (Le Guin's crowding); accidental repetition is the target (Browne & King, "once is usually enough").
- -ly adverbs and dialogue tags: look hardest at adverbs on tags and at ornate tags (Browne & King). A plain count of adverbs is the simple-rule approach writers criticise in AutoCrit.
- Filter words, crutch words, was+ing: treat as places to read, like ProWritingAid's narrow named reports. Nothing in the research sets a threshold for them.
- Set the genre first (AutoCrit lesson); what counts as a problem depends on it.
- Check the style sheet's "author style, keep" entries before flagging. Fragments, odd punctuation and long sentences are often deliberate.
- Compare against the book's own baseline, not a bestseller average (regression to the mean).
- Run the counts before any model call: cheap, deterministic, explainable.

## Volume cap
- Floods kill trust: one ProWritingAid user found about one real problem per thirty flags (tildes.net thread); Lex users accept under half of brevity and readability flags (adopter.substack.com).
- Cap findings per pass, rank by severity, deliver the top ones first. No source gives a number; the cap is editwright's choice.
- Let the author pick which checks run; explain every suggestion (Lex).
- Keep the suggestion list the source of truth; formats are views of it ([delivery.md](delivery.md)).

## Replacement limits
A replacement is allowed only for the smallest span (typo, comma, tense slip, cut, reorder) and must reuse the author's words. Anything needing new wording is a query. Counting rules: [ai-text-rules.md](ai-text-rules.md).
